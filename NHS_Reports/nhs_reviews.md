# What major reviews have the NHS or the royal college of obsterticans and gynecologists undertaken since 2010 on improving materntiny care and outcomes?

Since 2010, the NHS and independent regulatory bodies have undertaken several major national and trust-specific reviews to address severe safety concerns and improve maternity care outcomes. These reports continually emphasize the need to address poor clinical cultures, improve collaborative working, and take patient feedback seriously when evaluating care standards.[^1][^2]

### Morecambe Bay Investigation

Published in 2015 by Dr. Bill Kirkup, this review examined avoidable harm within the maternity services at the Morecambe Bay NHS Trust. The investigation identified a long-standing refusal to properly engage with patient experiences and noted missed opportunities for intervention at nearly every level of the NHS.[^2]

### National Maternity Review

The 2016 "Better Births" report by NHS England established a national vision for maternity services, recommending that care become more personalized, safer, and family-centered. It emphasized the necessity of accreditation systems for care providers and proposed personal maternity care budgets to give women greater control over their birthing decisions.[^3][^4]

### Ockenden Review

Donna Ockenden's 2022 report investigated catastrophic systemic failures at the Shrewsbury and Telford NHS Hospital Trust, where families were ignored by staff for over two decades. The review discovered that the maternity governance team deliberately downgraded serious safety incidents to evade external scrutiny, prompting immediate mandates for enhanced workforce planning and transparent investigations.[^2]

### East Kent Maternity Review

Dr. Bill Kirkup published another major independent report in 2022 focusing on systemic maternity failures at the East Kent Hospitals University NHS Foundation Trust. The review concluded that at least 45 babies could have survived if they had received acceptable clinical care, underscoring a continuous failure to learn from past tragedies.[^5]

### Nottingham Maternity Inquiry

Currently the largest maternity inquiry in NHS history, Donna Ockenden is reviewing approximately 2,500 cases of inadequate care at Nottingham University Hospitals, with a final report expected in June 2026. Families demanded this independent review after initial internal reports were deemed insufficient, and the police concurrently launched a corporate manslaughter investigation in 2025 regarding the trust's potential gross negligence.[^6]

### National Maternity Investigation

In March 2026, Baroness Amos published the interim report for a system-wide independent investigation into maternity and neonatal services across England. Triggered by the persistent recurrence of trust-level scandals, this rapid national inquiry aims to establish comprehensive actions to elevate safety, leadership, and clinical standards across all English maternity units.[^7][^6]
